### 1. Best reconstruction of the decision

Morgan Lee must walk into each four-week pricing review with a **per product-at-store recommendation** for the California pilot store’s everyday grocery and household aisles: **raise the shelf price**, **take a targeted price cut**, or **leave the shelf price unchanged**. When the historical evidence is not solid, the legitimate call is **“hold — not enough evidence”**, not a forced raise/cut/hold.

The right call is the action expected to bring in the **most product revenue** (units × shelf price) over the **next 28 days**, compared with leaving the current price in place. A revenue-improving move is still rejected if it is expected to cut that product’s units by **more than about 10%**. Price cuts that raise units are acceptable if revenue holds.

Only information that would actually be known at the review date may be used: own daily unit sales, weekly shelf prices, and calendar (holidays/events and SNAP days). No cost/margin, inventory, competitor prices, or look-ahead. Historical price/sales patterns support an **honest expectation to be piloted**, not a guarantee; whatever the store GM signs off is piloted, not rolled out blind.

The team can work up **about 25 actual price-change recommendations** (raises or cuts) per cycle. Output is a **ranked list**. The top trusted items, up to about 25, form this cycle’s GM package. Further qualifying raise/cut items stay **below the line as next in line** and do not enter this cycle’s package. Do not pad if fewer than 25 qualify. Hold and hold-not-enough-evidence items do not consume the 25 but must remain listed so they were visibly reviewed. “Strongest” means the **biggest revenue gain the analysis actually trusts**.

First-round eligibility principle (cutoffs not locked): several real past shelf-price changes and sales steady enough that a change would show; prefer a smaller list that can be trusted over a large speculative one. Do not guess on products whose price has never moved or that barely sell.

Recommender: Morgan. Approver: store general manager.

### 3. Available actions or choices

For each in-scope product at the specific store:

- Recommend **raising** the shelf price  
- Recommend a **targeted price cut**  
- Recommend **leaving the shelf price unchanged** (current price is the right call)  
- Classify as **“hold — not enough evidence” / can’t-tell** (explicit legitimate outcome; not a forced call)

Capacity applies only to the first two (actual changes). Holds of both kinds are listed, not counted against ~25.  
Supported by T002, T016, T020.

### 4. Intended outcome

**Maximize expected product revenue** — units sold × shelf price — **over the 28 days after the review**, at the recommended price versus the current price. Margin is the preferred commercial number but is unavailable; revenue is what Morgan can defend with the GM.  
Forward-looking expectation is required, not only a recap of last year.  
Supported by T004, T012, T014.

### 5. Material constraint

Several stakeholder-owned constraints, in the order they were stated:

1. **Volume guardrail:** reject a move expected to cut the product’s units by **more than about 10%**, even if revenue rises (T006).  
2. **Evidence / uncertainty:** fewer recommendations with stronger evidence; weak evidence → hold-not-enough-evidence rather than a forced change; prefer a missed opportunity to a bad change walked into the GM’s office (T016).  
3. **Information boundary:** only internal history known as of the review day — daily units, weekly shelf prices, calendar (holidays/events, SNAP). No cost, inventory, competitor prices, or look-ahead (T012, T014).  
4. **Capacity:** about **25 actual price changes** (raise or cut) per four-week cycle; ranked list; top ~25 = this cycle’s package; remainder below the line as next in line; no padding; holds listed but excluded from the cap (T018, T020, T024).  
5. **Eligibility principle:** several real historical shelf-price changes **and** steady enough sales; smaller trustworthy list; no guessing on never-moved or barely-sold items. Exact cutoffs delegated with disclosure (T008, T010).  
6. **Implementation / epistemic:** approved changes are **piloted, not rolled out blind**; past patterns are not proof; present honest expectation, not a guarantee (T026).  
7. **Grain / first-round scope:** product at a specific store; one California pilot store; everyday grocery and household aisles (exact departments open to suggestion) (T008).

### 6. Time scope or deadline

- Recurring **four-week pricing review**; the set price holds until the next review.  
- Outcome window Morgan cares about: the **next four weeks / 28 days**.  
- For this exercise: treat the review as occurring **at the end of the most recent available history**; use only what would have been known that day.  
Supported by T012 (and T000 “next pricing review”).

### 7. Evidence that would change the action

From stakeholder wording, the action flips when:

- Expected **unit decline > about 10%** → do not take the raise/cut to the GM, even if revenue is ahead (T006).  
- Evidence on the product is **not solid** → “hold — not enough evidence,” not a forced raise/cut/hold (T016).  
- Expected **28-day revenue at the candidate price does not beat current price** (and a cut does not at least hold revenue) → leave unchanged (T004, T006).  
- Product has **no real price-movement history or barely sells** → out of first-round scope / do not guess (T008, T010).  
- **More than ~25** trusted raise/cut opportunities exist → only the top trusted revenue gains enter this cycle’s package; the rest wait below the line (T018, T024).  
- A recommendation is dressed as a **guarantee** of what the new price will do → Morgan would not treat it as usable; it must be an expectation for a pilot (T026).  
- Information that would **not have been known** on the review day (look-ahead, cost, inventory, competitors) cannot support the call (T012, T014).

### 8. Strongest remaining ambiguity

**The action is specified only as direction, not magnitude.** “Raise the shelf price” and “take a targeted price cut” are never given a size, step, or candidate new shelf price. “Targeted” is Morgan’s word (T002) but is undefined. Scoring of “biggest revenue gain you actually trust,” exact eligibility cutoffs, exact aisle/department list, and the “about 10%” / “about 25” approximations are explicitly delegated — those belong in Framing / Stage 3. The magnitude gap is the only **action-set** hole still inside Start: the three-way (plus can’t-tell) choice can be assigned without saying *how far* the shelf price should move.

Secondary, procedural only: T027 itself has not been confirmed (T027 is held for this review).

- **Pilot implementation is under-stated** — T026 is two claims: (1) GM-signed changes are **piloted, not rolled out blind**, and (2) do not dress history up as a guarantee. T027 keeps only the epistemic half. The pilot/rollout half is omitted.

- **No fabricated outcome or fake constraint** — T027 does not invent margin, competitor data, multi-store rollout, or a hard 25-item quota that must be filled.

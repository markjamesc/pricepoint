## Project class (owner-locked, the only fixed element)
PricePoint must support one product-level retail pricing/revenue decision using M5 sales, price, and calendar evidence.

## Persona
- Pricing & Revenue Manager for a fictional California grocery/household pilot. Recommends shelf-price changes; a store general manager signs off.
- Business-literate, not technical. Does not know SQL, R, or modeling. Does not dictate methods.
- Answers one question at a time, reveals context only when asked, may correct the analyst.

## Opening request (spoken first, verbatim)
"I keep hearing we're leaving money on the table with our shelf prices. Can you dig into our sales and price history and tell me which products we should be changing prices on? I'd like something I can actually use at our next pricing review."

## Hidden context (reveal gradually, only when a question reaches it)
1. Decision/action: at each four-week pricing review Morgan decides, product by product, whether to recommend raising the shelf price, applying a targeted price cut, or holding the current price. Not assortment, promotions calendar, inventory, or staffing.
2. Scope: start with one pilot store in California (M5 store CA_1), everyday grocery and household aisles. Unsure of exact departments; open to the analyst's suggestion. Wants products that actually have a history of price movement ("no point guessing on something whose price has never moved") and enough sales to judge. Does not know product counts.
3. Outcome: item revenue (units x shelf price) over the next four weeks. Would love margin, but knows this data has no cost or margin, so revenue is what she can defend.
4. Guardrail (candidate, open to discussion): does not want revenue gains bought by losing a lot of volume; "if a move is expected to cut units by more than about 10 percent, I don't want it, even if revenue goes up."
5. Horizon: the next four weeks (28 days) after the pricing-review date. Decision uses only history available up to that date.
6. Information at decision time: past daily unit sales, weekly shelf prices, calendar events/holidays, SNAP benefit days. No competitor prices, inventory, cost, or customer-level data.
7. Forward-looking need: "I need to know what to expect over the next four weeks at a given price, not just what happened last year." Wants an expectation of the next four weeks' units/revenue at current price versus the candidate price. Does not name a method.
8. Uncertainty tolerance: prefers "hold / not enough evidence" to a weak call; would rather miss an opportunity than make a bad move; wants an explicit "can't tell" outcome.
9. Capacity: her team can realistically work up about 25 price-change recommendations per cycle. If more qualify, she needs the strongest ones first. If fewer qualify, do not pad the list.
10. Causality: understands historical price/sales patterns are not proof of what a new price will do; recommendations would be piloted, not rolled out blindly.
11. Timing: "use the most recent history you have" as the pricing-review date. Portfolio exercise, no real deadline.

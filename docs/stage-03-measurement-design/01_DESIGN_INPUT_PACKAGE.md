## A. Business inputs (locked in Stages 1–2)

**Original request (T000, verbatim):** "I keep hearing we're leaving money on the table with our shelf prices. Can you dig into our sales and price history and tell me which products we should be changing prices on? I'd like something I can actually use at our next pricing review."

**Approved decision statement (Stage 1, locked):**
> Morgan Lee, Pricing & Revenue Manager, must determine what recommendation to bring to the store general manager for products meeting the review criteria at the California pilot store’s everyday grocery and household aisles: a specific pilot shelf price increase, a specific pilot shelf price cut, leaving the shelf price unchanged, or classifying the product as “hold — not enough evidence” when the available evidence is not solid. The recommendation should identify the specific pilot price expected to produce the strongest trusted product revenue result (units sold × shelf price) over the next 28 days compared with the current price, while rejecting changes expected to reduce units by more than about 10%. The review occurs on a four-week cycle, with the price decision holding until the next review. The recommendation uses only information available at the end of the most recent history period: daily unit sales, weekly shelf prices, and calendar information including holidays, events, and SNAP benefit days. The output is a ranked list: the strongest trusted price-change opportunities, based on the biggest revenue gain that is trusted, form the GM package up to about 25 actual price changes. If fewer qualify, the list is not padded. If more qualify, additional qualifying opportunities remain below the line as next in line and are not included in that cycle’s package. Holds and hold — not enough evidence outcomes remain visible and do not consume the price-change capacity. Approved changes are pilots, and historical patterns should be presented as expectations to test rather than guarantees.

**Locked analytical question (Stage 2, C2):**
> For products meeting the review criteria in the California pilot store’s everyday grocery and household aisles, which specific pilot shelf prices should Morgan recommend for the next 28-day review cycle to produce the strongest trusted expected product revenue (units sold × shelf price) compared with the current price, without recommending a change expected to reduce units by more than about 10%; which products should instead remain unchanged or be classified as “hold — not enough evidence”; and which trusted price-change opportunities rank into the current-cycle GM package of up to about 25 actual price changes, with any additional qualifying opportunities listed below the line?

**Decision owner:** Morgan Lee (Pricing & Revenue Manager) recommends; the store general manager signs off.

**Available actions per product:** a specific pilot shelf price increase; a specific pilot shelf price cut; leave the shelf price unchanged; "hold — not enough evidence".

**Outcome:** expected product revenue (units sold × shelf price) over the next 28 days at the candidate price, compared with the current price. No cost/margin data exists.

**Constraints:**
- Unit guardrail: reject any change expected to reduce units by more than about 10%. A cut that raises units is fine if revenue holds.
- Capacity: `capacity_stance = hard_attention_budget`. The capacity unit is actual raise or cut recommendations per four-week cycle, about 25. Holds and hold — not enough evidence outcomes do not consume capacity but must be listed. No padding if fewer qualify. Extra qualifiers are listed below the line as next in line and are not in this cycle's package.
- Trust is an evidence **gate**, not a weight: untrusted expectations become "hold — not enough evidence". Among trusted opportunities, rank by the biggest expected revenue gain. Trust = the price has actually moved before, the product sells enough to see what happened, and the forward four-week expectation is not a guess. No stakeholder minimum-gain threshold; any minimum-gain rule is an analyst choice that must be disclosed.
- Eligibility principle (stakeholder): exclude products whose price has never moved or that barely sell; the product should have changed price "at least a few times"; cutoffs are the analyst's to set and disclose. Prefer a smaller trusted list.
- Grain: product at a specific store. Scope: one California pilot store (M5 `CA_1` is the candidate mapping) and the "everyday grocery and household aisles"; the department mapping is open to analyst proposal (the stakeholder is not sure which departments).
- Time: four-week review cycle; the decision holds 28 days. Treat the review as happening at the end of the most recent history (last observed sales day d_1941 = 2016-05-22). Use only information known as of that day: daily unit sales, weekly shelf prices, calendar (holidays, events, SNAP days). No competitor prices, inventory, or cost.
- Output form: specific pilot shelf price per recommended change, with expected units and revenue at that price and at the current price; ranked list with the GM package (up to about 25) and a below-line list; holds listed.
- Interpretation: approved changes are pilots; historical patterns are expectations to test, not guarantees. The candidate-price construction method is the analyst's choice, to be shown.
- Project requirement (owner): the design must use a predictive model in R with tidyverse (panel construction, joins, wrangling) and parsnip (the ML model specification). Because the capacity list is ranked by expected gain, and the use test in framework §17A/FORWARD says any model value that changes a judged action or capacity ranking before Validation freeze is Mode A, **ml_mode A is expected**; Mode B may not rank the capacity list. Rules-only Mode None is out of scope for this project.

## B. Data inputs

**Database:** MySQL 8.0, schema `pricepoint`, read-only. Stage 4 source delivery is SQL through the mysql command-line client; one frozen bounded extract is written, verified by the SQL Source Gate, and the identical package is fed to R-A and R-B. **R never connects to MySQL.** R version 4.6.1. No staging objects may be created in the database.

**Tables (all value columns are stored as varchar unless noted):**

1. `raw_sales_evaluation` — 30,490 rows, one per product-store series (3,049 items × 10 stores). Columns: `id` (unique; e.g. `FOODS_1_001_CA_1_evaluation`), `item_id`, `dept_id`, `cat_id`, `store_id`, `state_id`, `sales_history` (JSON array of exactly 1,941 daily unit-sales integers; position 0 = d_1 = 2011-01-29, position 1940 = d_1941 = 2016-05-22). Hierarchy: state → store → category → department → item. Zeros in sales are not a stockout diagnosis (no inventory data). Items may have leading zeros before they were stocked.
2. `raw_calendar` — 1,969 rows, one per day 2011-01-29 to 2016-06-19. Columns: `date`, `wm_yr_wk` (Walmart week key; weeks run Saturday–Friday), `weekday`, `wday` (coding Sat=1 … Fri=7, not Mon=1), `month`, `year`, `d` (d_1…d_1969), `event_name_1`, `event_type_1`, `event_name_2`, `event_type_2` (types Cultural/National/Religious/Sporting; blank is normal), `snap_CA`, `snap_TX`, `snap_WI` (0/1).
3. `raw_sell_prices` — 6,841,121 rows. Grain store_id × item_id × wm_yr_wk; `sell_price` weekly (overall mean ~$4.41, min $0.01, max $107.32). Not balanced: a product-store has price rows only for weeks it was on sale. Join only on store_id + item_id + wm_yr_wk. A missing price is never zero. Discounts are inferred from price changes, not labeled promotions.

**Profiled facts (read-only aggregates, 2026-09-25; `artifacts/framing/feasibility_probe_01.*` and `artifacts/design/design_profile_01.*`):**
- `raw_sell_prices` key (store_id, item_id, wm_yr_wk) is unique: 6,841,121 rows = 6,841,121 distinct keys.
- `raw_sales_evaluation.id` is unique (30,490); `JSON_LENGTH(sales_history)` is 1,941 for every row.
- `raw_calendar`: 1,969 distinct `d`, 282 distinct `wm_yr_wk`; weeks have 7 days except the last week 11621 (2 days: 2016-06-18/19). 162 days have event_name_1, 5 have event_name_2, 650 are snap_CA days.
- Week 11616 = 2016-05-14 to 05-20. **Week 11617 = 2016-05-21 to 05-27, which straddles the decision date** (last sales day 2016-05-22). The 28-day horizon is d_1942–d_1969 = 2016-05-23 to 2016-06-19 (weeks 11617–11621).
- `raw_sell_prices` includes prices for horizon weeks 11617–11621 (after the decision date). Using prices after the decision date would be look-ahead, except for the price in effect at the decision date.
- Horizon sales (d_1942–d_1969) are not in this data. Any backtest/validation must use an earlier pseudo-decision date.
- CA_1 departments (items; items with any price change; items with 4+ distinct prices; avg priced weeks): FOODS_1 216/180/69/241.5; FOODS_2 398/363/202/228.0; FOODS_3 823/583/209/226.9; HOUSEHOLD_1 532/367/63/220.1; HOUSEHOLD_2 515/310/38/237.0. (HOBBIES_1 416 and HOBBIES_2 149 items also exist at CA_1.)
- CA_1 price ranges by dept: FOODS_1 $0.97–12.98; FOODS_2 $0.64–13.98; FOODS_3 $0.20–19.48; HOUSEHOLD_1 $0.01–29.97; HOUSEHOLD_2 $0.75–26.88. No blank prices.
- Every CA_1 item has a price row in week 11616 and in week 11617. At CA_1, no item has an internal gap in weekly price rows between its first and last priced week (price rows are contiguous once an item starts).
- Horizon calendar (2016-05-23 to 06-19): MemorialDay (National) 05-30; NBAFinalsStart (Sporting) 06-02; Ramadan starts (Religious) 06-07; NBAFinalsEnd (Sporting) and Father's day (Cultural) 06-19; snap_CA = 1 on 06-01 through 06-10.
- A bounded extract for CA_1 and 3–5 departments is feasible (≈1.5M item-day rows after pivot for 748 items; well within R memory).

**Unknowns (not yet profiled; request targeted profiling if a design decision depends on them):** per-item sales-volume distributions and zero-day shares; distribution of number of price changes per item before the decision date; typical price-change magnitudes.

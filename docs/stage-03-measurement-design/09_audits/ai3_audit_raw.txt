Tolerances feasibility for two independent glmnet fits. With identical folds (ceiling(5·d/D); live/backtest/stability cut points as in RES-06), identical 50-value grid 10^seq(−4, 1, length.out = 50), identical tie rule (larger penalty), identical recipe (with recipe-side standardization and glmnet standardize = FALSE), identical feature construction (RES-05), identical target winsorization (RES-05/§15), and identical seed 20160522, glmnet's fit is deterministic given the input matrix and the fold assignments. The remaining difference between R-A and R-B is floating-point order of operations and the resulting CV RMSE comparison, which can produce different penalties only when two grid values are nearly tied. When the CV-RMSE gap between the top two penalties is very small, R-A and R-B may select adjacent grid indices. |Δ| ≤ 0.05 units on 28-day sums and $0.05 on revenue are loose enough that adjacent grid indices with near-tied CV error should still fall inside tolerance for daily-unit predictions in the item ranges seen at CA_1 (P02 P4: mean |pct change| 16.22%; sums of 28 days in the range of tens to thousands of units). If the two paths select penalties whose difference exceeds a factor of 10, actions can flip. Non-determinism is not a tolerance problem, it is a rule-completeness problem; RES-06's tie rule (larger penalty) removes most of it. Keep penalty_grid_index as diagnostic (already in v4) and require that any recon FAIL caused by adjacent-index selection be repaired toward the spec, not averaged. This is already in v4.

Gap where predictions within tolerance flip an action at a cent-rounding or 0.90 boundary. Yes: R-A and R-B can both satisfy |Δ .pred_units_current| ≤ 0.05 and |Δ delta_rev| ≤ $0.05 and still produce different actions if the R-A value sits just above a boundary and the R-B value sits just below, in one of these places:

ρ̂ = 0.90 boundary (guardrail).

round(R̂(Pc),2) − round(R̂(P0),2) = 0.00 boundary (cent-rounding of the legal test, per 06 Appendix A step 7).

argmax ΔR̂ boundary when two candidate ΔR̂ values differ by ≤ $0.10 (twice the revenue tolerance).

rank boundary when adjacent qualifiers' delta_rev differ by ≤ $0.10, and the tie-break fields pred_units_current / n_price_changes_pre are equal, so ordering falls to item_id ascending (deterministic and equal across paths, so this case is safe).

package_flag boundary at rank 25 when delta_rev of ranks 25 and 26 differs by ≤ $0.10.

Exact required rule to add to §23 (new sentence): "When a continuous-tolerance check passes but any action, package, below-line or rank field disagrees, reconciliation FAILS and both paths repair toward the spec independently; tolerance is never used to reconcile a discrete field. Where a candidate is within the revenue tolerance of a rule boundary (ρ̂ within ±0.001 of 0.90; round(R̂(Pc),2) − round(R̂(P0),2) within ±$0.01 of 0.00; two candidate `delta_rev` values within $0.10 of each other at the argmax; delta_rev of ranks 24–26 within $0.10), R-A and R-B must emit a boundary_flag on the affected item, but the judged action remains the one produced by the locked rule." NON-BLOCKING but required for a well-formed recon.

## T3 — OD-1 (TE6, "the forward four-week expectation is not a guess")

Decision: **(ii) ADOPT WITH AMENDMENT.** The coordinator's 06#8 rule is the correct construct; the amendment is confined to closing the determinism gaps that R-A and R-B would otherwise have to invent.

Rationale from the SQL→R lens (3–8 lines):
No evidence at Stage 3 could ground any specific APE cap: no model fits exist, and framework §25 bars production analysis before lock. The 0.50 cap is therefore a disclosed coordinator proposal, and the honest choice is to keep it as a proposal — locked because reconciliation requires one, not because evidence supports it. The model-level collapse (06#28) remains the population-level control: if median APE > 0.40 or bias exits [−0.20, +0.20], every trust-eligible item is hold_ne, so the 0.50 item-level cap only binds when the model as a whole has already been accepted; it is a per-item tail, not a substitute. The dept-median branch is defensible but fragile: with few usable items the median is unstable, and adding one item can flip an existing item's TE6; a floor of 5 usable items before the branch is applied keeps it deterministic. "No usable slice" must be defined or Stage 4 invents one; 06#8 currently does not. Finally, three emitted fields (`te6_ape_i`, `te6_dept_median_ape`, `te6_rule_fired`) are needed for §23 to test TE6 exactly, and none appears in the current v4.

Exact amended rule (replaces the RES-09 body text):

> TE6 passes iff the item has a usable d_1913 backtest slice AND (APE_i ≤ 0.50 OR APE_i ≤ the median APE over usable items in its department), where:
> - A usable slice means: E_A true when re-anchored at d_1913 (n_price_changes ≥ 3 at weeks ≤ 11613; units_365 over d_1549–d_1913 ≥ 180; zero_days_365 over the same window ≤ 182; priced_weeks ≥ 52 at weeks ≤ 11613); the price is the same in weeks 11613, 11614, 11615, 11616, 11617; and realized units d_1914–d_1941 > 0.
> - APE_i = abs(Û_i(P0_bt) − U_i) / U_i, where P0_bt = week-11613 price, Û_i is the 28-day summed prediction over d_1914–d_1941, U_i is realized units d_1914–d_1941.
> - If fewer than 5 usable items exist in the item's department, the dept-median branch is not applied; only the absolute 0.50 cap decides that item's TE6.
> - Items without a usable d_1913 slice fail TE6 → action = hold_ne.
> Emit `te6_ape_i`, `te6_dept_median_ape`, `te6_usable_slice ∈ {0,1}` and `te6_rule_fired ∈ {absolute_only, dept_median_only, both, neither}` on the universe row; these four fields are reconciliation-exact per §23. If the audits converge on this amendment, OD-1 becomes Locked-proposed at lock; otherwise OD-1 becomes an owner choice at lock per 06 §F.

Note on the interaction with 06#28: TE6 is evaluated at d_1913 for all universe items in the R pipeline regardless of whether the model collapses. If `backtest_accept = 0`, every trust-eligible item becomes hold_ne by §17.R8 (Appendix A step 8); TE6's value in that state is only a diagnostic. The design must state this ordering so R-A and R-B do not differ on whether TE6 is computed for items whose trust collapses: §17.R6 must be extended with "te6 is computed before the §17.R8 collapse test; the collapse rule then overrides actions and does not delete the te6 field."
